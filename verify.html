  01Understand what you are approvingSignIn, NFT minting, registry publication, and confirmation.
  


A standard Xaman SignIn authenticates an account without submitting a transaction to the XRP Ledger. It is not itself a payment or an NFT mint, and it does not approve later transactions on your behalf.


A later request may authorize an actual ledger transaction. Read it inside Xaman before approving. Check the transaction type, selected account, network, amount, destination where applicable, NFT fields, and fee. Do not sign a request you do not understand—even when the website looks familiar.



  
Connecting identifies the account.
SignIn is an authentication step. It should not be confused with creating or publishing a prayer NFT.

  
Minting creates the NFT.
NFTokenMint creates a non-fungible token. In this version, the signed mint transaction also contains the prayer text in public memos.

  
Full publication has two approvals.
After the mint validates, the separate one-drop registry transaction links the NFT and prayer record to the shared feed. The Prayer Map re-reads the validated registry and applies its display thresholds.

  
Signing is not final confirmation.
For a ledger action, successful completion requires a successful result in a validated ledger. A signature, pending screen, or provisional result is not final confirmation.




Technical costs are not a price on prayer. Ledger transactions have network costs, and NFT ownership can affect reserve requirements. Review the current request and any separately disclosed charges. These technical conditions do not measure spiritual value.


Never enter your seed phrase, Secret Numbers, recovery phrase, or private key into this website or send them to someone offering support. Those secrets can give another person control of your account.

  02What is a digital relic?Remembrance and witness—not spiritual status.
  


A keepsake of faith—not a certificate of holiness.


For JCS, digital relic is a devotional description of a contemporary digital artifact: a prayer, testimony, or remembrance associated with a ledger record.


Think of it as a keepsake of a moment of faith. It may help someone remember what they prayed, what they were thankful for, or why they chose to speak about Christ.


The term does not mean that the NFT is an ancient object, an authenticated relic of a saint, or an object with supernatural power. It does not certify holiness, confer salvation, or make one person’s prayer more important than another’s.


Its intended meaning is remembrance and witness—not spiritual status.



  
What does the record establish?
An NFT provides a unique ledger identifier and a record that can be examined independently. Its history can establish technical facts about the token and related transactions—not the truth of every associated statement.
“I believe this prayer was answered” is a participant’s testimony. Recording it does not independently prove a miracle, divine intervention, or a person’s spiritual condition.

  
Where is this page’s prayer stored?
In this version, the complete prayer and its SHA-256 commitment are included in the public mint transaction memos. A separate registry transaction is used for full publication. New prayer NFT URIs point to shared JCS artwork metadata. The individual prayer is in the mint memos; wallet artwork does not contain a separate hosted copy of every prayer.
The referenced image is separate from the ledger history. A token’s existence does not guarantee that an image, website, or public history provider will remain available.




A digital artifact of Christian prayer and remembrance, associated with a verifiable ledger record. A witness to what you chose to express—not a certificate of spiritual truth.


Read the Verification Evidence Center and the page’s existing mint and registry explanation. Protocol reference: NFTokenMint and its URI.







  03Share thoughtfully. Protect one another.Public mint memos, wallet visibility, and voluntary map focus.
  


Share a witness, not someone’s private life.


Public XRP Ledger transactions can be inspected by others. A wallet address is not the same as a person’s name, but public activity and the details someone shares may allow others to connect the two. Do not assume a wallet-linked post is anonymous.


Before publishing, remove information that could expose you or someone else: full names, exact addresses, children’s identifying details, confidential confessions, private medical information, or the location of a vulnerable person or congregation. Ask permission before sharing another person’s identifiable story.


Mint-only does not mean private. This version writes the prayer into the mint transaction memos. Hiding a wallet in the interface and suppressing a map aggregate do not remove the underlying signed transaction from public history.



  
Publication is a choice.
You may pray without publishing. A private prayer is not less sincere because it does not appear on a wall, receive responses, or become an NFT.
Jesus’ teaching in Matthew 6 distinguishes sincere prayer from religious performance done for an audience. Give yourself space to decide whether these words should become public.

  
Protect vulnerable people.
A broad location or a shortened name is not a promise of anonymity. The Prayer Map’s optional focus describes the place a prayer concerns—not the signer’s location.
When public expression could put someone at risk, choose private prayer rather than a public record.




Before you continue


Review the exact words and details you are about to publish. Treat anything placed in a public transaction as information that may remain accessible. Removing it from a website or burning the NFT does not erase the historical transaction or retrieve copies already made by others.


A personal review: “I understand that this action may create a public record. I have reviewed the content and removed information that could expose me or another person.”


This is reading guidance, not an additional consent control or a claim that consent has been recorded. The existing wallet review and map-focus confirmation remain unchanged. Browser drafts are not a confidential or encrypted vault; use care on shared devices.

  05Fellowship, replies, and encouragementRespond honestly. Respect privacy. Let care extend beyond a comment.
  
Pray with care. Respond with kindness.
Behind a prayer request is a person—not a statistic, a wallet balance, or an opportunity to promote something.
Read with compassion. Respond honestly. Do not assume you know why someone is suffering or tell them that hardship proves a lack of faith. Where you can offer appropriate practical help, let your concern extend beyond a comment.
Galatians 6 emphasizes carrying one another’s burdens, while Romans 12 connects Christian community with sincere love, hospitality, generosity, and perseverance in prayer.
Responses you can adapt
Use a response only when it honestly reflects your intention. Copying a response is not an on-ledger Amen or reply.
For someone asking for strength
I will keep this concern in prayer today. May you find strength for the next step and trustworthy support beside you.
For someone sharing grief
Thank you for sharing this remembrance. I am sorry for your loss, and I will pray for comfort for you and those who loved them.
For a testimony of gratitude
Thank you for sharing what this has meant to you. May your gratitude become encouragement for someone facing a difficult season.
When you do not have an answer
I do not have an explanation for what you are experiencing, but I can listen and pray with you.
Let an “Amen” express sincere encouragement. A response count is not a measure of anyone’s faith or the importance of their prayer.
A prompt for a later testimony
What has changed since you first prayed about this? What are you thankful for? What remains difficult? Describe what you personally experienced, and distinguish what you know from what you believe or hope.
Community boundaries
Do not use prayer requests to pressure people for money, wallet access, private contact details, or public disclosure. Do not promise guaranteed healing, wealth, or spiritual favor. Respect another person’s decision not to share more.

  06Learn, pray, and reflectShort readings on prayer, testimony, community, and remembrance.
  


Prayer is not a performance.
Prayer does not require polished language, a public audience, or an impressive story. In Matthew 6, Jesus teaches prayer without making public recognition its purpose and gives His followers a model centered on God, daily needs, forgiveness, and deliverance.
Begin with what is true: what you are thankful for, what troubles you, where you need guidance, and whom you want to remember. A few sincere words can be a beginning.


Why pray together?
Christian fellowship makes room for shared prayer, learning, encouragement, and practical care. Acts 2 describes believers gathering around teaching, prayer, shared meals, and attention to one another’s needs.
An online prayer space can support those habits, but it should point toward real concern for people. A thoughtful response matters most when it comes from someone willing to listen, remain present, or help responsibly.


What is a testimony?
In this community, a testimony is your account of what you have experienced and how you understand it in relation to your faith.
Speak honestly and in your own voice. You do not need to exaggerate suffering or claim certainty you do not possess. “I believe,” “I experienced,” and “I am still trying to understand” can all be truthful beginnings. A testimony may include gratitude and unresolved difficulty at the same time.


When the outcome is not what you hoped for.
You do not need to manufacture an uplifting ending. You may say that you are still waiting, disappointed, confused, or grieving.
This community should make room for honest uncertainty rather than rewarding only stories with a clear resolution. Seek trustworthy people who can listen without blaming you. Let prayer accompany appropriate practical support and professional care—not become a reason to refuse them.


Remembering without turning faith into a competition.
A digital keepsake may help you remember a prayer or a meaningful moment. It should not become a ranking system for holiness.
JCS’s stated principles reject wealth-based spiritual ranking and token-weighted theology. A person’s dignity must not rise or fall with their holdings, activity, or visibility. Let the record serve remembrance. Let your treatment of others express what matters.
