Vision and core values

This is a living statement of intent and responsibility. It is not a technical white paper, securities offering, promise of profit, church charter, or claim of spiritual authority.

Christ first · technology second

The mission is Christ; the technology is a tool. JCS exists to carry testimony about Jesus, preserve prayer and Scripture references in durable digital form, support voluntary fellowship, and make public project activity easier to verify.

Every system should protect human dignity, disclose its limitations, minimize private-data exposure, preserve user-controlled authorization, and remain useful even to a visitor who never buys, holds, or trades JCS.

Christ centered

Features should direct attention toward Jesus Christ rather than speculation, personalities, or technological novelty.

Human dignity

Prayer, grief, crisis, disability, poverty, persecution, and testimony must never become engagement bait.

Voluntary participation

No prayer, testimony, NFT, transaction, reaction, or public record should occur without a deliberate user action.

Non-custodial design

Public transparency

The issuer, token identity, AMM, order books, transactions, and public records should be independently inspectable.

Privacy restraint

Public evidence should contain the minimum necessary information. Sensitive communication belongs off the public ledger.

Accessible witness

Core text should remain usable without sound, animation, a wallet, a token balance, or a high-powered device.

Stewardship over hype

Utility, truth, maintenance, safety, and service take priority over unsupported price, adoption, or partnership claims.

Safety, privacy, and people at risk

Christian expression can carry personal risk. A public blockchain is durable and observable, so technical permanence must never be confused with confidentiality.

Safety before publicity

Silent access

Written Scripture, prayer references, safety guidance, and the mission must remain usable without audio, video, or animation.

Identity restraint

Names, locations, handles, medical details, legal matters, and identifying stories should not be placed on-ledger when disclosure could cause harm.

Public versus private

An NFT or transaction can create public evidence of an event. Private support, pastoral discussion, and sensitive communication require separate protected channels.

  • Public ledger warning: XRPL accounts, transactions, trust lines, offers, AMMs, NFTs, and memos may remain publicly observable indefinitely.
  • No secret requests: JCS will never ask for a seed phrase, private key, recovery words, Xaman passcode, or device passcode.
  • No emergency service: JCS is not an emergency, medical, mental-health, legal, law-enforcement, or crisis-intervention service.
  • No spiritual ranking: Token holdings, NFT ownership, transaction count, donations, reactions, or account age do not establish faith, authority, truth, or worth.

Public evidence. Private dignity.

The project now uses two deliberately different information paths. Public XRPL evidence is useful for chronology and verification; sensitive human communication should remain off-ledger unless a user deliberately publishes a minimal neutral fingerprint.

Architecture boundary

Public evidence plane

Designed for information that can safely be durable and independently reconstructed.

  • Validated XRPL transactions and ledger sequence
  • JCS issuer/currency identity and market structure
  • Prayer/testimony registry records deliberately published by the wallet holder
  • Prayer NFT receipt and ledger-derived Prayer ID
  • Thresholded Prayer Map cohorts and aggregate support relationships
  • Hashes, protocol versions, machine-readable metadata and evidence exports

Private dignity plane

Designed to avoid turning personal need into a permanent public record.

  • Local prayer journal and local drafts where implemented
  • Private Capsule plaintext and recovery keys remain outside public map metrics
  • Encrypted Capsule transport can be separated from its key card
  • Exact GPS, IP, browser/device and relay data are not accepted as prayer-location evidence
  • Operational escape routes, safe-house locations and vulnerable third-party identities should never be published
  • Endpoint compromise can defeat browser encryption; the system does not claim impossible confidentiality
Rule for future features

Do not put information on a public blockchain merely because it can be hashed, tokenized or minted. First decide whether the user benefits from permanent public evidence. If the information could endanger, embarrass, identify or expose a person, the safer default is local or protected processing with the smallest possible public receipt.

How these metrics are calculated and what they cannot prove
  • All XRPL requests specify or rely on validated-ledger data when the method supports it.
  • Order-book amounts use taker_gets_funded and taker_pays_funded when present, falling back to the full offer only when the offer is not partially funded.
  • Trust-line retrieval is paginated but capped to reduce load. A capped sample is labeled rather than represented as complete.
  • Wallet addresses are not verified real-world identities, and multiple addresses may be controlled by one party.
  • Price, spread, depth, pool value, holder count, and indicative network value can change before another person loads the page.
  • No metric proves faith, truth, legality, solvency, intent, ownership, future price, or future project success.
